 🍄 Isolation of Fungi from Textile Effluent and Production of Bio-compost for Seed Germination

 📌 About the Project

Textile industries generate large quantities of wastewater containing dyes, organic pollutants, salts, heavy metals and other chemicals. Improper discharge of untreated textile effluent can cause serious environmental pollution and affect water quality, soil fertility, aquatic organisms and agricultural productivity.

This mini project focuses on the *isolation of fungi from textile effluent and evaluation of their potential for fungal bioremediation. The study also investigates the effect of biologically treated textile effluent on the germination and early growth of **green gram (Vigna radiata)* seeds.

The fungal isolate was identified as *Aspergillus spp.* based on colony morphology and microscopic examination using Lactophenol Cotton Blue (LPCB) staining.



 🎯 Aim

To isolate fungi from textile effluents and evaluate their ability to degrade pollutants while studying their effect on seed germination and plant growth.



🎯 Objectives

- To isolate fungi from textile effluent.
- To identify fungal strains capable of degrading pollutants present in textile effluent.
- To evaluate the role of isolated fungi in promoting seed germination.
- To utilize fungal strains for the bioremediation of contaminated textile wastewater.
- To explore an eco-friendly and cost-effective approach for textile wastewater treatment.



 🧪 Study Area

The textile wastewater sample was collected from:

*Agraharam, Erode, Tamil Nadu, India*

The collected effluent was used for fungal isolation, biodegradation studies and evaluation of fungal-treated effluent on green gram seed germination.



 🦠 Fungal Isolation and Identification

Textile effluent samples were serially diluted and inoculated onto suitable culture media.

Fungal colonies were isolated and purified for identification.

The fungal isolate was identified as:

*Aspergillus spp.*

Identification was carried out using:

- Colony morphology
- Microscopic examination
- Lactophenol Cotton Blue (LPCB) staining

The observed fungal characteristics included septate hyphae, conidiophores, vesicles, phialides and chains of conidia.

---

 🔬 Experimental Methodology

 1. Textile Effluent Collection

Textile wastewater was collected from Agraharam, Erode, Tamil Nadu, in sterile containers and transported to the laboratory.

 2. Serial Dilution

The effluent sample was serially diluted to obtain suitable microbial concentrations for isolation.

 3. Fungal Isolation

The diluted samples were inoculated onto Malt Extract Agar (MEA) plates and incubated at suitable conditions for fungal growth.

 4. Identification

Distinct fungal colonies were purified and examined microscopically using LPCB staining.

 5. Culture Preparation

The selected Aspergillus isolate was maintained using suitable culture media and developed as an inoculum for the degradation study.

 6. Fungal Treatment of Textile Effluent

The Aspergillus culture was used to treat textile effluent at different concentrations:

- 20%
- 40%
- 60%
- 80%
- 100%

The inoculated effluent was incubated under controlled conditions.

 7. Purification of Treated Effluent

The treated effluent was centrifuged and the supernatant was collected for further study.

 8. Seed Germination Study

Green gram seeds (*Vigna radiata*) were used to evaluate the effect of treated textile effluent.

Different effluent concentrations were tested:

- Control
- 20%
- 40%
- 60%
- 80%
- 100%

Seed germination and early plant growth were observed and compared.

 9. SDS-PAGE Analysis

SDS-PAGE was performed to study the protein profile of the isolated Aspergillus species.

Distinct protein bands were observed after staining with Coomassie Brilliant Blue R-250.



📊 Results
Fungal Isolation

Distinct fungal colonies were successfully isolated from textile wastewater.
 Fungal Identification

The purified fungal isolate was identified as *Aspergillus spp.* based on morphological and microscopic characteristics.

 Textile Effluent Treatment

Treatment of textile wastewater with the isolated Aspergillus species resulted in a noticeable reduction in colour intensity, indicating the potential biodegradation of dye compounds.
 Seed Germination

Green gram seeds treated with different concentrations of fungal-treated textile effluent showed differences in germination and early growth.

Better germination, root length and shoot length were observed at lower concentrations, while higher concentrations showed some reduction in seed growth.

 SDS-PAGE

SDS-PAGE analysis showed distinct protein bands in the isolated Aspergillus culture, supporting the study of its protein profile and biodegradation potential.


 🌱 Importance of the Study

This project combines *environmental biotechnology and agricultural applications*.

The study demonstrates the potential of indigenous fungal isolates for:

- Textile wastewater bioremediation
- Reduction of textile effluent toxicity
- Biological treatment of industrial wastewater
- Supporting sustainable wastewater management
- Studying the effect of treated wastewater on seed germination



♻️ Environmental Significance

Fungal bioremediation provides a biological approach to wastewater treatment.

Fungi can produce extracellular enzymes such as:

- Laccase
- Lignin peroxidase
- Manganese peroxidase
- Cellulase

These enzymes can contribute to the degradation of complex organic pollutants and dyes.
